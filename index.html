Governing Flow Equationsformulas with descriptions
Incompressibility\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=0\]

Meaning: fluid is not created or destroyed inside the tunnel. Whatever enters a small region must leave it. Here, \(u\) and \(v\) are the horizontal and vertical velocity components.

Momentum Conservation\[\rho(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u}\]

Meaning: this is the core Navier-Stokes force balance. Fluid motion carries momentum, pressure pushes the flow, and viscosity smooths sharp velocity changes. The dashboard uses this idea to explain why wakes and boundary layers matter.

Reynolds Number\[Re=\frac{\rho U L}{\mu}\]

Meaning: \(Re\) compares inertia to viscosity. Low \(Re\) tends to be smoother and easier for the PINN to learn. High \(Re\) tends to create stronger separation, turbulence, and delayed grokking.

CFL Time-Step Guardrail\[\Delta t \le C_{\max}\frac{\Delta x}{\|\mathbf{u}\|+a}\]

Meaning: the time step must be small enough that information does not jump too far in one update. \(\Delta x\) is cell size, \(\|\mathbf{u}\|\) is flow speed, \(a\) is wave speed for compressible flow, and \(C_{\max}\) is the stability limit.

Reliability Quantitieschecks with descriptions
Mass Conservation Error\[\epsilon_m=\frac{\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|}{\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS}\]

Meaning: this checks whether total inflow and outflow balance across the boundary \(\partial\Omega\). A real CFD result should keep this value very small before claiming accuracy.

Drag and Lift\[C_D=\frac{F_D}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2A},\qquad C_L=\frac{F_L}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2A}\]

Meaning: \(C_D\) and \(C_L\) turn drag and lift forces into dimensionless numbers. This lets different shapes, wind speeds, and sizes be compared fairly.

Wall Resolution\[y^+=\frac{\rho u_{\tau}y}{\mu}\]

Meaning: \(y^+\) measures whether the mesh is fine enough near walls to resolve the boundary layer. If it is too large, wall shear, drag, and near-wall turbulence estimates become unreliable.

Residual Convergence\[\mathcal{R}^{(n)}=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}^{(n)}-\mathbf{b}\|_2\]

Meaning: the residual measures how far the numerical solution is from satisfying the discretized equations. In real CFD, residuals should drop and stabilize before the output is trusted.

Project Reportplain-language summary
Scientific Lab Report

Title

Physics-Informed Neural Network Grokking in Simplified Fluid Dynamics. This project studies how a neural network can first memorize flow behavior and then suddenly generalize after enough training. The dashboard uses a browser-based wind-tunnel surrogate, interactive controls, scientific plots, AI equations, and physics equations to explain that process.

Abstract

The goal of the project is to demonstrate the connection between fluid-flow complexity and delayed generalization in physics-informed neural networks, or PINNs. The dashboard compares a ground-truth flow surrogate with a PINN-style prediction and shows how training loss, validation accuracy, wake profiles, Reynolds number, turbulence intensity, and spectral bias relate to grokking. The main result is that simple flows generalize sooner, while separated or turbulent wakes delay validation improvement because the model must learn finer spatial structure.

Research Question

How do geometry, wake complexity, collocation density, and shared representation learning affect the epoch at which a physics-informed neural network begins to generalize?

Hypothesis

If the flow is smooth or geometrically simple, then the model should generalize earlier because low-frequency structure is easier to learn. If the body creates strong separation, high drag, or turbulent wake features, then grokking should occur later because the model must represent higher-frequency details.

Important Limitation

This is an educational dashboard, not a production CFD solver. The flow field is a fast visual surrogate. It is useful for explaining concepts, but real engineering claims would require a conservative finite-volume solver, geometry cleanup, adaptive meshing, mesh independence, residual convergence, and validation against benchmark data.

Method and Variableslab-report structure

Method

The experiment is run as an interactive simulation study. First, a geometry is selected and placed in the wind tunnel. Next, training settings such as epoch, collocation density, and joint versus separate learning are adjusted. The dashboard then compares the visual ground-truth field with the PINN-style predicted field and updates the graphs that describe loss, accuracy, wake profiles, spectral behavior, and reliability warnings.

Independent Variables

  • Geometry: circle, ellipse, wedge, plate, or wing.
  • Training mode: separate learning or joint shared-representation learning.
  • Epoch: training time from the beginning to late training.
  • Collocation fraction: how strongly the model is checked against physics points.
  • Angle, wind velocity, wake drag, flow option, turbulence model, and mesh level: advanced controls that change flow difficulty and reliability warnings.

Dependent Variables

  • Validation loss and accuracy: whether the learned field generalizes.
  • Snap epoch: when grokking occurs.
  • Wake-profile agreement: how closely the predicted velocity profile matches the truth curve.
  • Spectral gap: how much high-frequency content the model still struggles to represent.
  • Reliability indicators: residual, CFL, mass error, y+, drag/lift, and validation status.

Controlled Variables

The dashboard keeps the domain, visual grid, base geometry definitions, and plotting framework consistent. This makes it easier to compare how shape and training settings affect the model behavior.

Research Graph Analysisloss, accuracy, and co-grokking

Figure 1: Grokking Loss and Generalization Accuracy

The first research figure has two panels. The loss panel uses a logarithmic vertical axis, so each tick mark represents a power of ten. This is useful because training loss and validation loss can change by many orders of magnitude. The training loss drops early, showing that the model can fit the training examples quickly. The validation loss stays high for much longer, which means the model has not yet learned a rule that works outside the examples it memorized.

The accuracy panel shows the same story from a different angle. Training accuracy reaches a high value early, but validation accuracy remains low until the grokking snap. The important scientific point is that low training error does not automatically mean real understanding. Generalization appears only after the internal representation becomes simpler and more physically useful.

Figure 2: Multi-Geometry Co-Grokking

The co-grokking graph compares separate training against joint training. In separate training, each geometry has its own snap epoch because each flow pattern is learned independently. In joint training, multiple shapes share a representation, so they can generalize around the same training time. This supports the idea that a model can learn a reusable flow language rather than a separate trick for each body.

Interpretation

Together, these graphs show the difference between memorization and generalization. Memorization is local and fast. Generalization is delayed because the model must discover a structure that works across unseen points, unseen flow regions, or even multiple geometries.

Wake and Turbulence Graph Analysisphysical flow complexity

Figure 3: Aerodynamic Wake Velocity Profiles

The wake profile graph compares velocity slices at downstream positions. The horizontal axis is normalized velocity \(u/U_{\infty}\), and the vertical axis is height \(y\). The CFD truth curve shows the expected wake deficit: velocity drops in the region behind the object. The memorizing PINN curve is intentionally noisy and poorly aligned, showing a model that has not yet learned the real wake structure. The grokked PINN curve nearly overlaps the truth curve, showing successful generalization.

The near wake is harder because the flow changes rapidly close to the body. The far wake is smoother but still contains important structure. A good PINN should recover both the broad velocity deficit and the shape of the profile.

Figure 4: Reynolds Number and Turbulence Effects

The Reynolds graph shows that more complex flow regimes require more epochs before grokking. Low Reynolds number flow is usually smoother. Higher Reynolds number flow can separate, shed vortices, and create smaller scales. These small scales increase the learning burden.

The spectral-bias panel explains why. Neural networks often learn smooth, low-frequency patterns first. Turbulent wakes contain high-frequency content, so the model needs more training before it can represent them well. This is why the spectral gap grows with turbulence intensity.

Scientific Meaning

The physical takeaway is that the AI difficulty is not random. It is tied to the structure of the fluid problem. Smooth attached flow is easier; separated turbulent flow is harder.

Diagnostics Graph Analysiswhat the live checks mean

Activation Spectrum

The activation spectrum shows which frequencies are present inside the model representation. A clean grokked model should concentrate power in meaningful low and mid frequencies. A memorizing model often has scattered or noisy high-frequency power because it is fitting details without a stable rule.

Collocation Scarcity Regime

Collocation points are the locations where the physics equations are checked. If there are too few points, the model can pass through the training data while violating physics elsewhere. If there are enough useful points, the physics residual guides the model toward a better field. The graph shows that grokking can be delayed when physics supervision is scarce.

Geometry Snap Comparison

This graph compares the snap epoch for each shape. Blunt or separated geometries usually snap later because their wakes are harder. Joint training lowers the gap by using shared structure across shapes.

Training Phase Map

The phase map summarizes training behavior. Early training is dominated by fitting and memorization. The middle phase forms a useful circuit or representation. The final grokking phase occurs when validation loss drops and the learned field becomes more physically consistent.

AI Equations ExplainedPINN and grokking
Network Prediction\[\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{\theta}(x,y,t,g)=\big(\hat{u},\hat{v},\hat{p}\big)\]

The neural network receives position \((x,y)\), time \(t\), and geometry label \(g\). It predicts the flow quantities: horizontal velocity \(\hat{u}\), vertical velocity \(\hat{v}\), and pressure \(\hat{p}\). The symbol \(\theta\) means the trainable weights of the network.

PINN Objective\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{physics}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{physics}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{bc}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bc}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\|\theta\|_2^2\]

This is the training score the model tries to minimize. It combines matching data, obeying physics, satisfying boundary conditions, and keeping weights controlled. The \(\lambda\) values are weights that decide how much each part matters.

Data Fit\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\|\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_i)-\mathbf{q}_i^{\mathrm{CFD}}\|_2^2\]

This measures how far the neural prediction is from reference CFD samples. If this term is small, the model matches known examples well. Matching examples alone is not enough, because the model can still fail between points.

Physics Residual\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{physics}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_j\|\mathcal{N}[\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{\theta}](\mathbf{x}_j)\|_2^2\]

This checks whether the predicted flow violates the governing equations. \(\mathcal{N}\) is the physics operator, such as the Navier-Stokes residual. If this loss becomes small, the prediction is more physically consistent.

Co-Grokking Representation\[z_{\theta}=\phi_{\theta}(x,y,t,g),\qquad \hat{\mathbf{q}}_{\theta}=h_{\theta}(z_{\theta})\]

The model first builds an internal representation \(z_{\theta}\), then converts it into flow predictions. Co-grokking means different shapes begin to share a useful internal flow language, so validation improves together rather than one shape at a time.

Spectral Bias Diagnostic\[G_{\mathrm{spectral}}=\frac{\|\hat{a}_k\|_{\mathrm{high}}^2}{\|\hat{a}_k\|_{\mathrm{low}}^2}\]

This compares high-frequency structure to low-frequency structure in the learned representation. Smooth large-scale patterns are low frequency. Small turbulent wake details are high frequency. Neural networks often learn smooth patterns first, which is why complicated wakes can generalize later.

Activation Spectrum\[\hat{a}_{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_n e^{-i2\pi kn/N},\qquad P_k=\frac{|\hat{a}_k|^2}{\max_j|\hat{a}_j|^2}\]

This uses a Fourier transform to break hidden activations into frequencies. \(P_k\) is the normalized power at frequency \(k\). In simple words, it shows whether the model is using smooth structure or noisy fine detail.

Collocation Scarcity\[E_{\mathrm{grok}}(f)\propto f^{-1.85}\]

Here \(f\) is the fraction of physics collocation points. \(E_{\mathrm{grok}}\) is the epoch where generalization snaps into place. The formula says that with fewer useful physics points, grokking tends to take longer.

Physics Equations Explainedflow and reliability
Incompressibility\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=0\]

This says fluid is not piling up or disappearing inside the domain. Whatever flows into a small region must flow out. It is the mass-conservation rule for incompressible flow.

Momentum Conservation\[\rho(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u}\]

This is the Navier-Stokes momentum equation in simplified form. The left side is fluid motion carrying momentum. The right side says pressure gradients and viscosity change that motion.

Reynolds Number\[Re=\frac{\rho U L}{\mu}\]

Reynolds number compares inertia to viscosity. Low \(Re\) usually means smoother laminar flow. High \(Re\) usually means stronger separation, turbulence, and harder learning.

CFL Time-Step Guardrail\[\Delta t \le C_{\max}\frac{\Delta x}{\|\mathbf{u}\|+a}\]

This says the time step must be small enough that information does not jump too far in one update. \(C_{\max}\) is the stability limit, \(\Delta x\) is cell size, \(\|\mathbf{u}\|\) is flow speed, and \(a\) is wave speed for compressible cases.

Mass Conservation Error\[\epsilon_m=\frac{\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS\right|}{\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}|\,dS}\]

This measures whether total inflow and outflow balance. A small value is good. A large value means the simulated flow is not conserving mass reliably.

Drag and Lift\[C_D=\frac{F_D}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2A},\qquad C_L=\frac{F_L}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2A}\]

These convert drag and lift forces into dimensionless coefficients. That makes forces easier to compare across different speeds, densities, and body sizes.

Wall Resolution\[y^+=\frac{\rho u_{\tau}y}{\mu}\]

\(y^+\) measures how well the mesh resolves the near-wall boundary layer. If it is too high for the chosen turbulence model, wall shear and drag estimates become unreliable.

Residual Convergence\[\mathcal{R}^{(n)}=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}^{(n)}-\mathbf{b}\|_2\]

The residual measures how far the current solution is from satisfying the discretized equations. A decreasing residual is a sign that the numerical solution is settling down.

Reliability Statementwhat can and cannot be claimed

The dashboard can show trends, intuition, and interactive relationships between geometry, wake complexity, grokking, and model diagnostics. It can estimate residual-like warnings, CFL risk, mass error, and force coefficients for teaching purposes.

It should not claim production CFD accuracy. A real accuracy claim would require imported and cleaned geometry, adaptive finite-volume meshing, a mesh-independence study, benchmark validation, pressure and wall-shear convergence, and documented residual and conservation checks.

Conclusion

The main conclusion is that PINN generalization depends on both learning dynamics and flow physics. A model can look successful on training examples before it truly understands the flow. The grokking snap happens when the model discovers a representation that is simple enough, physical enough, and reusable enough to work beyond memorized samples.

The graphs support this conclusion from several directions. Loss and accuracy show the delayed validation transition. Wake profiles show the physical difference between memorization and a useful prediction. Reynolds and turbulence graphs explain why complex wakes delay learning. Diagnostics show how representation quality changes internally. The architecture and equation pages connect those observations to the PINN training objective.

Future Work

The next step would be connecting this dashboard to a real CFD backend. That backend would import external geometry, clean the mesh, run adaptive finite-volume solves, perform mesh-independence studies, and validate against benchmark cases. The current dashboard is best understood as a polished scientific explanation and interactive prototype.
